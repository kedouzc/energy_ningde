## Digital Battery Passport by 2027: The Infrastructure Race is On

Starting February 18, 2027, every electric vehicle battery, light means of transport battery, and industrial battery greater than 2 kWh sold in the European Union must carry a digital battery passport accessible through a QR code. This requirement represents the most comprehensive traceability mandate ever imposed on battery manufacturers globally. Unlike collection targets or recovery efficiency requirements that measure end-of-life performance, the battery passport creates permanent digital infrastructure documenting battery identity throughout its entire lifecycle from manufacture through recycling. For manufacturers, this infrastructure requirement creates strategic opportunity: companies deploying passport systems now establish data advantage competitors cannot replicate when implementation deadlines arrive.

## Understanding the Digital Battery Passport Mandate

[From 18 February 2027, all](https://www.circularise.com/blogs/eu-battery-passport-regulation-requirements) industrial rechargeable batteries with capacity greater than 2 kWh, batteries for electric vehicles and those for light means of transport must be accompanied by digital battery passports accessible via QR code. This mandate applies to all applicable batteries regardless of final product destination. A battery installed in a vehicle, energy storage system, or consumer device must carry the passport.

[The digital battery passport](https://www.flashbattery.tech/en/blog/eu-battery-regulation-obligations-updates/) is an electronic document designed to track and provide access to key information about batteries throughout their entire life cycle. The passport functions as permanent digital record following the battery from production through use into recycling or second-life deployment. This traceability transforms battery management from static compliance exercise into continuous data collection and reporting process.

[From February 18, 2027,](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-us/resource-centre/blogs/mobility-and-automotive/understanding-the-new-eu-battery-regulation) LMT, EV, and industrial batteries with capacity greater than 2 kWh must be electronically registered with a battery passport carrying an identification QR code and CE marking. Electronic registration creates legally documented baseline establishing initial battery state, composition, and manufacturing specifications that subsequent users and recyclers access throughout the battery's lifespan.

## QR Code Infrastructure: Establishing Unique Battery Identity

[QR codes serve as](https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/platform/sites/default/files/2024-03/1qp5rxiZ-CEPS-InDepthAnalysis-2024-05_Implementing-the-EU-digital-battery-passport.pdf) unique product identifiers linking to battery passport records, with economic operators responsible for establishing and maintaining the QR code and the underlying battery identification system. The QR code creates connection point between physical battery and digital data record, enabling immediate access to battery information at any point in the product lifecycle.

Manufacturers must implement systems capable of generating unique QR codes for individual batteries, printing or engraving codes on batteries or product packaging, and maintaining linkage between QR codes and digital records throughout battery lifespan. This infrastructure requirement extends beyond simple barcode generation to include data management systems ensuring code accessibility, accuracy, and persistence across years of operation.

[The battery passport must](https://www.s-ge.com/en/article/spotlight/2025-e-ct10-introduction-eu-battery-passport) be created via certified platform and made available digitally, with access enabled through QR code attached directly to the battery or product. Certified platform requirement establishes baseline standards for data security, information accuracy, and system reliability ensuring passport data meets regulatory verification requirements.

## Data Elements: What the Battery Passport Contains

[For the initial 2027](https://www.circularise.com/blogs/eu-battery-passport-regulation-requirements) battery passport, only basic battery identification, type, model, and key technical characteristics are required, with updates on lifecycle performance and durability statistics potentially mandated in future delegated acts but not legally required at first implementation stage. This phased approach enables manufacturers to establish baseline infrastructure without requiring complete data collection systems before February 2027 deadline.

Essential data elements include battery identification and unique identifier, battery type and model designation, production date and manufacturing facility, chemical composition and material specifications, carbon footprint calculation and verification, and technical performance characteristics including durability and charge cycle capacity. [The Battery Passport has](https://www.informatica.com/resources/articles/digital-product-passport-battery-passport-guide.html) more specific data requirements than a generic Digital Product Passport, such as detailed material composition, carbon footprint calculations, and state-of-health tracking.

[From 18 February 2027,](https://cms-lawnow.com/en/ealerts/2025/11/eu-sustainable-batteries-regulation-where-are-we-now) all batteries must bear a QR code giving access to prescribed, up-to-date information including capacity, chemistry and certain substances, the EU declaration of conformity and end-user/distributor information on prevention and management of waste, with EVs, LMT and industrial rechargeable batteries providing access to the digital battery passport centralizing model and battery-specific data including carbon footprint, recycled content, durability/performance parameters and test reports using role-based access to protect commercially sensitive information.

## Role-Based Access: Protecting Competitive Information While Enabling Transparency

The passport establishes tiered data access controlling which stakeholders view specific information based on their role in the battery lifecycle. Public data includes battery identification, model designation, chemistry, and recycling instructions accessible to all users. Recycling operator access includes state-of-health information, material composition, degradation curves, and processing requirements enabling optimized recovery workflows. Regulatory authority access includes full technical specifications, manufacturing details, supply chain documentation, and proprietary process information. Original equipment manufacturers access warranty and performance data supporting product support decisions. This role-based structure balances transparency requirements with intellectual property protection ensuring manufacturers can share necessary information without exposing proprietary technology or competitive trade secrets.

Manufacturers implementing passport systems must establish sophisticated access control infrastructure ensuring different user categories receive appropriate data visibility while maintaining security and preventing unauthorized access. Recycling operators need detailed material composition, electrochemical performance curves, and processing requirements enabling process optimization. Regulatory authorities require full technical documentation supporting compliance verification and market surveillance. OEMs and end-users access basic information supporting safe use, expected performance, and end-of-life management decisions. This multilayered data structure requires sophisticated data management platform capability differentiating advanced manufacturers from competitors lacking access control sophistication.

[The digital battery passport](https://cms-lawnow.com/en/ealerts/2025/11/eu-sustainable-batteries-regulation-where-are-we-now) uses role-based access to protect commercially sensitive information while ensuring all stakeholders can access data relevant to their responsibilities. Manufacturers can document proprietary manufacturing processes while sharing material composition with recyclers and performance data with regulators. This controlled transparency creates competitive advantage for manufacturers willing to share data through structured access frameworks.

## Carbon Footprint Declaration: Foundational Data Element

[Carbon footprint specific to](https://www.circularise.com/blogs/eu-battery-passport-regulation-requirements) the manufacturing site and batch of batteries is required, with the JRC methodology for calculating and verifying carbon footprints requiring site-specific and batch-level data for each manufacturing facility. Carbon footprint calculation creates baseline environmental metric enabling regulators to establish performance standards and manufacturers to demonstrate environmental commitment.

Site-specific and batch-level carbon data enables regulatory authorities to establish carbon footprint performance classes creating competitive incentive for manufacturers to reduce production emissions. First manufacturers demonstrating superior carbon performance gain customer preference and regulatory advantage. [Until carbon footprint information](https://www.flashbattery.tech/en/blog/eu-battery-regulation-obligations-updates/) is accessible via QR code from 18 February 2027, the declaration must accompany the battery, requiring manufacturers to begin carbon data collection immediately despite passport implementation occurring in 2027.

## Recycling Data Integration: Connecting Passport to Circular Supply Chain

[The battery passport provides](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-us/resource-centre/blogs/mobility-and-automotive/understanding-the-new-eu-battery-regulation) data on battery handling instructions and state of health to recycling operators and those repurposing batteries, enabling facilities to optimize processing and recovery workflows. Recycling operators accessing passport data know exact material composition, manufacturing chemistry, and performance degradation enabling process optimization that operations without passport access cannot achieve.

[Advanced battery recycling operations](https://www.greenli-ion.com/post/step-by-step-guide-to-the-li-ion-battery-recycling-process) integrating passport data into processing workflows achieve superior recovery rates through chemistry-specific process optimization compared to facilities processing batteries without composition documentation. Manufacturers partnering with passport-integrated recycling operations gain competitive advantage through documented recovery rates and cost-efficient processing.

Passport data enables recycling facilities to predict recovery rates with confidence, document compliance with recovery targets, and optimize material separation processes. Batteries arriving at facilities with complete passport data available can execute recovery workflows refined specifically for documented chemistry. This optimization advantage compounds across thousands of processed batteries, creating cost efficiency and recovery rate advantages unavailable to facilities without integrated passport systems.

## State-of-Health Information: Enabling Second-Life Applications

The battery passport includes state-of-health data tracking charge-discharge cycles, capacity degradation, and performance characteristics. This information enables second-life assessment determining whether batteries remain suitable for original application or qualify for lower-demand applications including stationary energy storage or grid services. [Batteries with sufficient remaining](https://www.greenli-ion.com/post/closed-loop-lithium-recovery) capacity can be deployed in second-life applications extending useful life and deferring recycling, reducing overall material demand and extending supply chain value.

Manufacturers implementing state-of-health tracking gain competitive advantage through documentation enabling second-life deployment qualification. Batteries with verified performance data access broader end-of-life markets including energy storage applications, grid services, and backup power systems. This expanded market access creates revenue opportunity and supply chain extension unavailable to manufacturers without documented state-of-health tracking.

Manufacturers deploying systems immediately complete infrastructure implementation within normal production timelines. Those delaying face compressed implementation schedules requiring disruption to manufacturing processes and quality assurance workflows.

Current preparation phase requires manufacturers to begin data collection system development, QR code generation infrastructure assessment, certified platform evaluation, and organizational training initiation. This gradual implementation enables systematic refinement and optimization before regulatory enforcement begins.

## Data Collection Challenges and Supply Chain Complexity

Passport implementation requires manufacturers to collect and verify data from complex multi-tier supply chains. Battery manufacturers source materials from mining operations, refining facilities, precursor material producers, and component manufacturers spanning multiple countries and regulatory jurisdictions. Each supply chain tier must provide documented material composition, environmental impact, and sourcing information feeding into passport records.

This supply chain transparency requirement creates operational challenge requiring manufacturers to establish supplier communication systems, data collection protocols, and verification procedures ensuring accuracy across independent suppliers. Manufacturers with mature supplier management systems and digital integration with key suppliers gain implementation advantage. Those managing suppliers through manual processes face significant data collection burden and compliance risk.

[Economic operators placing batteries](https://www.circularise.com/blogs/eu-battery-passport-regulation-requirements) on the market bear responsibility for verifying and maintaining passport information accuracy throughout battery lifespan. This responsibility extends beyond manufacturing to include data validation and updating as supply chain information becomes available or battery performance metrics change through operational use.

## Certified Platform Requirement: System Infrastructure Standards and Vendor Selection

[The battery passport must](https://www.s-ge.com/en/article/spotlight/2025-e-ct10-introduction-eu-battery-passport) be created via a certified platform and made available digitally, with responsibility for accuracy and completeness lying with the distributor placing the battery on the EU market for first time. Certified platform requirement establishes standardized infrastructure ensuring data quality, system security, and regulatory compliance across all manufacturers.

Manufacturers must evaluate and select certified platforms meeting regulatory standards for data management, security, accessibility, and interoperability. This platform selection creates competitive dynamic: manufacturers selecting robust platforms gain operational advantage through reliable data management and system scalability. Those selecting platforms lacking capability face integration challenges, performance limitations, and compliance risk.

Early platform selection enables manufacturers to integrate systems with existing IT infrastructure, establish staff training programs, and develop operational procedures before regulatory enforcement. Manufacturers delaying platform selection until deadline approaches face vendor capacity constraints, rushed implementation, and potential platform limitations limiting competitive advantage.
